## 1. Meaning and Scope of Geography

Geography studies places, environments and relationships between people and the physical world. Physical geography examines natural systems; human geography studies populations, settlements, economic activity and spatial patterns. Maps and fieldwork help gather and interpret evidence.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 2. The Earth and Its Movements

Earth rotates on its axis, producing day and night, and revolves around the Sun. Earth’s axial tilt combined with revolution causes seasonal changes. Latitude measures angular distance north or south of the Equator; longitude measures east or west of the Prime Meridian.

## 3. Weather and Climate

Weather describes atmospheric conditions over a short period; climate summarises patterns over many years. Temperature, rainfall, humidity, pressure and wind are key elements. Latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, ocean currents and relief influence climate.

## 4. Rocks and Landforms

Igneous rocks form from cooled magma or lava; sedimentary rocks form from deposited material; metamorphic rocks change under heat and pressure. Weathering breaks rock down in place, erosion transports material, and deposition lays it down.

## 5. Rivers and Drainage

A drainage basin is the area drained by a river and its tributaries. Upper courses commonly have steep gradients and vertical erosion; lower courses often have wider valleys, meanders and floodplains. Discharge depends on factors including rainfall, basin size and surface conditions.

## 6. Population and Settlement

Population distribution describes where people live; density is population divided by land area. Birth rate, death rate and migration affect population change. Settlements may be rural or urban, and their patterns are influenced by water, relief, transport, jobs and services.

## 7. Economic Activities and Resources

Primary activities extract or harvest resources; secondary activities process materials; tertiary activities provide services. Resource use depends on accessibility, technology, demand and sustainability. Economic activities often cluster near transport routes, markets, labour and raw materials.

## 8. Maps, Scale and Fieldwork

A map scale relates map distance to ground distance. At a scale of 1:50,000, 1 cm on the map represents 50,000 cm (500 m) on the ground. Contour lines join points of equal elevation. Fieldwork should use a clear question, reliable observations and careful recording.

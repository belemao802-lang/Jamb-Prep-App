# History — Topic-by-Topic Lessons

## 1. Meaning and Sources of History

History studies and interprets past human events using evidence. Primary sources originate from the period or participants being studied; secondary sources interpret or analyse evidence later. Historians compare sources, context, authorship, purpose and reliability.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 2. Pre-colonial African Societies

African societies developed diverse political and economic systems, including kingdoms, empires, city-states and decentralised communities. Their organisation varied by region and period; avoid treating Africa as one uniform society.

## 3. Trade and State Formation

Trade routes supported the exchange of goods, ideas, technology and religion. Control of trade, taxation, agriculture, military organisation and leadership could contribute to state formation, while conflict and environmental change could weaken states.

## 4. European Contact and the Atlantic Slave Trade

European contact varied across time and region. The Atlantic slave trade forcibly transported millions of Africans and reshaped communities and economies across Africa, Europe and the Americas. Study its causes, methods, consequences and resistance using reliable historical evidence.

## 5. Colonial Rule and Resistance

Colonial rule imposed foreign political and economic authority through different systems and policies. African responses included armed resistance, diplomacy, protest, adaptation and political organisation. Case studies should be examined in their local context.

## 6. Nationalism and Independence

Nationalist movements sought self-government through political parties, newspapers, unions, protests, negotiations and sometimes armed struggle. Economic change, education, international developments and post-war politics influenced decolonisation.

## 7. Post-independence Challenges

New states faced tasks such as nation-building, economic development, constitutional government and managing diversity. Outcomes differed by country; explanations should consider institutions, leadership, external pressures and local conditions.

## 8. Historical Evidence and Chronology

Chronology arranges events in time. Cause explains why an event occurred; consequence explains what followed. Strong historical answers distinguish evidence from opinion and consider multiple causes rather than assuming one event had only one explanation.

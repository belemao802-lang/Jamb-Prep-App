## 1. Meaning and Basic Concepts

Economics studies how people and societies allocate scarce resources among competing wants. Scarcity means resources are limited relative to wants; choice is therefore necessary. Opportunity cost is the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 2. Demand

Demand is the quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices over a period. Other things equal, quantity demanded usually rises when price falls. Income, tastes, population, expectations and prices of related goods can shift demand.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 3. Supply

Supply is the quantity producers are willing and able to offer at different prices. Other things equal, a higher price tends to encourage a larger quantity supplied. Production costs, technology, taxes, subsidies and weather can shift supply.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 4. Price Determination

Market equilibrium occurs where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. A price above equilibrium may create a surplus; a price below equilibrium may create a shortage. Changes in demand or supply can alter equilibrium price and quantity.

## 5. Production and Factors of Production

Production creates goods and services or adds utility. The main factors are land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship, each receiving a corresponding reward in simplified models: rent, wages, interest and profit.

## 6. Market Structures

Perfect competition has many buyers and sellers and relatively easy entry; monopoly has a single dominant supplier with high barriers to entry. Monopolistic competition features differentiated products, while oligopoly has a few interdependent large firms.

## 7. Money, Banking and Inflation

Money serves as a medium of exchange, unit of account and store of value. Commercial banks accept deposits and provide payment and lending services. Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level, reducing the purchasing power of money.

## 8. National Income and Public Finance

National income measures economic activity using concepts such as GDP. Fiscal policy uses taxation and public spending to influence the economy. A budget sets out expected public revenue and expenditure; deficits occur when expenditure exceeds revenue.

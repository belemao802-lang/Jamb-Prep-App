# Literature in English — Topic-by-Topic Lessons
**Status:** Original starter teaching pack. Check the exact topic list against the current official JAMB syllabus and prescribed texts before treating this pack as complete.

## 1. Literature and Genres

Literature uses language imaginatively to explore human experience. The major genres are prose, poetry and drama. Analyse what the text says, how it is constructed and how its choices create meaning; support interpretations with evidence.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 2. Prose: Plot and Structure

Plot is the arrangement of events in a narrative. Exposition introduces the setting and characters; conflict creates tension; climax is the turning point; resolution shows how the main conflict ends. Flashback and foreshadowing alter how readers receive information.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 3. Characterisation

Characterisation is how a writer presents a character through speech, action, thoughts, appearance and other characters’ reactions. A protagonist is central to the action; an antagonist opposes the protagonist’s aims. Characters may be dynamic or static.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 4. Setting and Theme

Setting includes time, place and social environment. Theme is a central idea explored by the text, such as power, betrayal, identity or justice. A theme is not merely a topic; explain what the text suggests about that topic.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 5. Poetry: Form and Sound

Study the speaker, situation, stanza, rhythm, rhyme, sound patterns and imagery. The speaker is the voice of the poem and is not automatically the poet. Form and sound can reinforce mood, contrast or emphasis.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 6. Figures of Speech

A simile compares using ‘like’ or ‘as’; a metaphor describes one thing in terms of another; personification gives human qualities to non-human things. Irony involves a contrast between expectation and reality, while hyperbole is deliberate exaggeration.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 7. Drama and Dramatic Techniques

Drama is written for performance. Dialogue reveals relationships and conflict; stage directions guide action and delivery. Dramatic irony occurs when the audience knows something a character does not. Soliloquies and asides can reveal private thoughts.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

## 8. Literary Criticism and Context

Interpret a text in its historical, cultural and literary context without assuming context alone determines meaning. Distinguish a narrator from an author, a theme from a plot, and a character’s opinion from the text’s overall position. Use precise textual evidence.

**Revision focus:** Define the key terms, explain the main principle in your own words, and apply it to an example or a JAMB-style question.

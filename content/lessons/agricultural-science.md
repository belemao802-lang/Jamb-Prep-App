# Agricultural Science — Topic-by-Topic Lessons
**Use:** Original JAMB-style revision notes; verify against the latest official syllabus.

## 1. Meaning, Scope and Importance of Agriculture
**Subtopics:** crop and animal production, food security, employment, raw materials, agricultural systems.
Agriculture includes crop production, livestock management, fisheries, forestry and related services. It supplies food and industrial raw materials and supports jobs and rural livelihoods. Subsistence farming mainly supports a household; commercial farming is oriented towards sale and profit.
**Check yourself:** Compare subsistence and commercial farming.

## 2. Agricultural Ecology
**Subtopics:** ecosystem, soil, climate, biotic and abiotic factors, ecological balance.
Crop and livestock performance depends on light, rainfall, temperature, soil, organisms and management. Biotic factors include pests and beneficial organisms; abiotic factors include water, pH and temperature. Sustainable management seeks productivity without degrading the resource base.
**Check yourself:** Identify limiting environmental factors for a crop.

## 3. Soil Formation and Profile
**Subtopics:** weathering, soil horizons, texture, structure, porosity, drainage.
Soil forms through weathering of parent material and addition of organic matter. A soil profile contains horizons with different mineral and organic content. Sand drains quickly; clay holds more water but may drain poorly; loam often offers a useful balance for cultivation.
**Check yourself:** Relate soil texture to water retention.

## 4. Soil Fertility and Conservation
**Subtopics:** macro- and micronutrients, manure, compost, fertilisers, erosion control, crop rotation.
Plants need nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium. Organic manure adds organic matter; fertilisers supply specified nutrients. Crop rotation, cover crops, mulching, contour farming and terracing can help reduce erosion and maintain soil quality.
**Check yourself:** Match common nutrient deficiencies to plant symptoms using a reliable reference.

## 5. Land Preparation and Tillage
**Subtopics:** clearing, ploughing, harrowing, ridging, tools and machinery.
Land preparation creates suitable conditions for planting. Tillage can loosen soil and control weeds but excessive tillage can increase erosion and moisture loss. Select tools according to scale, crop and soil conditions.
**Check yourself:** Distinguish ploughing from harrowing.

## 6. Crop Propagation and Planting
**Subtopics:** seeds, viability, germination, nursery practices, vegetative propagation, spacing.
Crops may be propagated by seed or vegetative methods such as cuttings, grafting and suckers. Germination requires viable seed and suitable water, oxygen and temperature. Correct spacing reduces competition and supports air circulation.
**Check yourself:** Give examples of crops propagated by seed and vegetative methods.

## 7. Crop Husbandry
**Subtopics:** planting, thinning, weeding, mulching, staking, pruning, irrigation and harvesting.
Good husbandry matches operations to crop growth stage. Weeds compete for resources; mulching conserves moisture and can suppress weeds. Harvest at suitable maturity to protect quality and reduce losses.
**Check yourself:** Explain why timing matters for weeding and harvesting.

## 8. Crop Classification and Uses
**Subtopics:** cereals, legumes, roots and tubers, vegetables, fruits, oil crops, fibre crops.
Cereals such as maize provide carbohydrate; legumes such as cowpea can enrich soil through symbiosis with nitrogen-fixing bacteria; roots and tubers store energy. Crop choice depends on climate, soil, market and intended use.
**Check yourself:** Classify common Nigerian crops by group and use.

## 9. Crop Pests and Diseases
**Subtopics:** pest types, symptoms, disease agents, cultural, biological and chemical control.
Pests damage crops directly or transmit disease. Disease may be caused by fungi, bacteria, viruses or other agents. Integrated pest management combines monitoring, cultural methods, biological controls and careful use of approved pesticides.
**Check yourself:** Distinguish a pest from a disease pathogen.

## 10. Weeds and Weed Control
**Subtopics:** weed identification, competition, life cycles, manual, cultural and chemical control.
Weeds compete with crops for light, nutrients, water and space. Control options include hand weeding, mulching, crop rotation and selective herbicides used according to label directions. Preventing seed production can reduce future weed pressure.
**Check yourself:** Explain two non-chemical weed-control methods.

## 11. Agricultural Machinery and Farm Tools
**Subtopics:** hand tools, tractors, planters, harvesters, maintenance and safety.
Mechanisation can improve timeliness and reduce labour demands. Machinery must match farm size and crop conditions. Routine maintenance, guarding, training and safe operation reduce accidents and downtime.
**Check yourself:** Match tools to farm operations.

## 12. Irrigation and Drainage
**Subtopics:** water sources, surface, sprinkler and drip irrigation, drainage, water conservation.
Irrigation supplies water when rainfall is insufficient; drainage removes excess water. Drip systems can deliver water near roots with reduced evaporation, while surface systems may be less expensive but can lose more water. Poor irrigation management may cause waterlogging or salinity.
**Check yourself:** Compare irrigation methods by cost and water efficiency.

## 13. Farm Animals and Their Classification
**Subtopics:** ruminants, non-ruminants, poultry, fish, breeds and production purpose.
Livestock include cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, poultry and fish. Ruminants have a multi-compartment stomach and microbial fermentation; poultry and pigs are non-ruminants. Choose breeds for climate adaptation, disease resistance and production goals.
**Check yourself:** Compare the digestive systems of ruminants and poultry.

## 14. Animal Nutrition
**Subtopics:** carbohydrates, proteins, fats, minerals, vitamins, water, ration and feed formulation.
Feeds provide energy, protein, minerals and vitamins. A balanced ration meets the animal's needs for maintenance, growth, reproduction or production. Ruminants use rumen microbes to digest cellulose more effectively than non-ruminants.
**Check yourself:** Explain why young and lactating animals may need different rations.

## 15. Animal Reproduction and Breeding
**Subtopics:** reproductive systems, oestrus, mating, artificial insemination, selection, crossbreeding.
Breeding programmes aim to improve useful inherited traits while maintaining health and adaptability. Selection chooses breeding animals with desired traits; crossbreeding combines characteristics of different breeds. Responsible management considers genetic diversity and welfare.
**Check yourself:** Distinguish selection from crossbreeding.

## 16. Animal Health and Disease Control
**Subtopics:** parasites, infectious diseases, vaccination, quarantine, hygiene, biosecurity.
Disease may be infectious, parasitic, nutritional or environmental. Biosecurity reduces the introduction and spread of pathogens through hygiene, isolation of sick animals and controlled movement. Follow veterinary guidance for diagnosis, vaccines and medicines.
**Check yourself:** Explain how quarantine protects a herd.

## 17. Livestock Management and Housing
**Subtopics:** housing, ventilation, stocking density, sanitation, handling, welfare.
Housing should protect animals from weather and predators while allowing ventilation, drainage and suitable space. Overcrowding raises stress and disease risk. Clean water, proper feed and humane handling are essential.
**Check yourself:** List features of a suitable poultry house.

## 18. Fisheries and Aquaculture
**Subtopics:** capture fisheries, fish ponds, stocking, feeding, water quality, harvesting.
Aquaculture raises aquatic organisms under managed conditions. Water quality, oxygen, stocking density, feed and disease monitoring influence yield. Overfishing and habitat damage threaten wild fish populations.
**Check yourself:** State factors that affect fish pond productivity.

## 19. Forestry and Agroforestry
**Subtopics:** forest products, afforestation, reforestation, agroforestry, conservation.
Forests provide timber, fuel, habitat and ecosystem services. Afforestation establishes trees on land not recently forested; reforestation restores tree cover. Agroforestry integrates trees with crops or livestock and may improve shade, soil protection and biodiversity.
**Check yourself:** Distinguish afforestation from reforestation.

## 20. Agricultural Economics and Farm Records
**Subtopics:** fixed and variable costs, revenue, profit, budgeting, depreciation, records.
Farm records support decisions and accountability. Profit = total revenue − total cost. Fixed costs do not vary directly with output over a short period; variable costs change with production level. Budgets estimate expected income and expenditure.
**Check yourself:** Calculate profit from given costs and sales.

## 21. Agricultural Marketing and Cooperatives
**Subtopics:** marketing channels, storage, transport, pricing, grading, cooperatives.
Marketing moves produce from farms to consumers. Poor roads, inadequate storage and price instability can increase post-harvest losses. Cooperatives may improve members' bargaining power and access to inputs or markets.
**Check yourself:** Identify stages in a crop's route to market.

## 22. Agricultural Extension and Rural Development
**Subtopics:** extension methods, demonstrations, farmer groups, communication, adoption of innovation.
Extension services share practical information and support farmers' decisions. Demonstrations, farm visits, group meetings and radio can communicate techniques. Recommendations should be adapted to local conditions and evaluated.
**Check yourself:** Explain how extension helps farmers adopt improved practices.

## 23. Post-Harvest Handling and Storage
**Subtopics:** drying, sorting, grading, storage pests, processing, preservation.
Drying reduces moisture and can limit microbial growth. Proper storage protects produce from pests, dampness and contamination. Processing can add value, but hygiene and safe handling are necessary throughout the supply chain.
**Check yourself:** Give methods to reduce grain storage losses.

## 24. Agricultural Policy, Challenges and Sustainability
**Subtopics:** credit, land tenure, labour, climate change, food security, sustainable practices.
Farmers may face constraints involving finance, land access, infrastructure, inputs, markets and climate variability. Sustainable agriculture combines productivity with soil and water protection, biodiversity, economic viability and responsible resource use.
**Check yourself:** Suggest practical responses to two local agricultural challenges.

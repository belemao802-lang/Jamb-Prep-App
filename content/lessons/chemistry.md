# Chemistry — Topic-by-Topic Lessons
**Use:** JAMB UTME revision. Original study notes; confirm the final topic list against the current official JAMB syllabus.

## 1. Nature of Matter and Separation Techniques
**Subtopics:** elements, compounds, mixtures, physical and chemical changes, filtration, crystallisation, distillation, chromatography, sublimation.
An element contains one type of atom; a compound contains elements chemically combined in fixed proportions; a mixture contains substances physically combined. Filtration separates an insoluble solid from a liquid. Simple distillation recovers a solvent from a solution; fractional distillation separates miscible liquids with different boiling points. Chromatography separates components by their different movement between stationary and mobile phases.
**Check yourself:** Choose a separation method for sand and water, salt solution, and ink.

## 2. Particulate Nature and Atomic Structure
**Subtopics:** atoms, molecules, protons, neutrons, electrons, atomic number, mass number, isotopes.
Protons carry +1 charge, electrons −1, and neutrons have no charge. Atomic number equals proton number; mass number equals protons plus neutrons. In a neutral atom, protons equal electrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with equal proton numbers but different neutron numbers.
**Check yourself:** Find the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in a neutral atom from its notation.

## 3. Periodic Table and Periodicity
**Subtopics:** groups, periods, metals and non-metals, atomic radius, ionisation energy, electronegativity, valency.
Elements in a group have related outer-shell electron arrangements and often similar chemical properties. Across a period, nuclear charge generally increases and atomic radius generally decreases; down a group, additional shells generally increase atomic radius. Trends have exceptions, so learn the explanations as well as the direction.
**Check yourself:** Explain why elements in the same group often react similarly.

## 4. Chemical Bonding
**Subtopics:** ionic, covalent and metallic bonding, dot-and-cross diagrams, properties of compounds.
Ionic bonding involves electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions after electron transfer. Covalent bonding involves shared electron pairs. Metallic bonding is attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons. Ionic substances often have high melting points and conduct electricity when molten or dissolved, but not as solids; simple molecular covalent substances often have lower melting points.
**Check yourself:** Relate bonding and structure to conductivity and melting point.

## 5. Chemical Formulae, Equations and Stoichiometry
**Subtopics:** valency, formula writing, balancing equations, relative atomic/molecular mass, mole, reacting masses, limiting reagent.
A chemical equation must conserve atoms and charge. Balance by changing coefficients, never subscripts. Relative molecular mass is the sum of relative atomic masses in a formula. Amount in moles: (n=m/M), where (m) is mass in grams and (M) is molar mass in g mol⁻¹. Use balanced equation coefficients as mole ratios.
**Check yourself:** Balance an equation and calculate moles from mass and molar mass.

## 6. States of Matter and Gas Laws
**Subtopics:** kinetic theory, diffusion, melting and boiling, pressure, Boyle's law, Charles' law, ideal gas equation.
Particles move continuously; temperature relates to average kinetic energy. At constant temperature, Boyle's law states (P_1V_1=P_2V_2). At constant pressure, Charles' law states (V_1/T_1=V_2/T_2), with temperature in kelvin. For an ideal gas, (PV=nRT) when units are consistent.
**Check yourself:** Convert Celsius to kelvin before gas-law calculations.

## 7. Solutions and Solubility
**Subtopics:** solute, solvent, saturated solutions, concentration, solubility curves, dilution.
A solution is a homogeneous mixture. Concentration in mol dm⁻³ is moles of solute divided by solution volume in dm³. Dilution reduces concentration without changing the amount of solute transferred: (C_1V_1=C_2V_2) for compatible units and a simple dilution. Solubility depends on substance and temperature; gas solubility often decreases as temperature rises.
**Check yourself:** Convert cm³ to dm³ and calculate molar concentration.

## 8. Acids, Bases and Salts
**Subtopics:** acid and base definitions, pH, indicators, neutralisation, salt preparation, acidic/basic salts.
Acids produce hydrogen ions in aqueous solution; bases neutralise acids, and alkalis are soluble bases that produce hydroxide ions. Neutralisation commonly forms salt and water. Use suitable methods to prepare salts: titration for soluble salts from acid and soluble alkali; excess insoluble base followed by filtration for many other soluble salts.
**Check yourself:** Select an appropriate method for preparing a soluble salt.

## 9. Oxidation and Reduction
**Subtopics:** oxygen and hydrogen transfer, electron transfer, oxidation numbers, oxidising and reducing agents.
Oxidation can mean electron loss or increase in oxidation number; reduction means electron gain or decrease in oxidation number. Oxidising agents accept electrons and are reduced; reducing agents donate electrons and are oxidised. In a redox reaction, oxidation and reduction occur together.
**Check yourself:** Identify what is oxidised and reduced in a simple reaction.

## 10. Electrolysis and Electrochemistry
**Subtopics:** electrolytes, electrodes, cations and anions, discharge, electroplating, Faraday principles.
Electrolysis uses electrical energy to drive a chemical change in an electrolyte. Cations move to the cathode and gain electrons; anions move to the anode and lose electrons. Products depend on ion concentration, electrode material and relative ease of discharge. In a simple electrochemical cell, chemical energy is converted to electrical energy.
**Check yourself:** Predict electrode products only after considering the electrolyte and electrode type.

## 11. Rates of Reaction
**Subtopics:** collision theory, concentration, pressure, surface area, temperature, catalysts.
A reaction occurs when particles collide with sufficient energy and suitable orientation. Higher temperature usually increases the fraction of particles with energy at or above activation energy. Greater concentration or gas pressure and larger solid surface area can increase collision frequency. Catalysts provide an alternative pathway with lower activation energy and are not consumed overall.
**Check yourself:** Explain each rate factor using collision theory.

## 12. Chemical Equilibrium
**Subtopics:** reversible reactions, dynamic equilibrium, Le Chatelier's principle, temperature, pressure and concentration.
At dynamic equilibrium in a closed system, forward and reverse reaction rates are equal and concentrations remain constant, though reactions continue. A system responds to a change in a way that tends to oppose it. Temperature changes can alter the equilibrium constant; catalysts speed both directions and do not change the equilibrium position.
**Check yourself:** Predict how a change affects a simple reversible reaction.

## 13. Energetics and Thermochemistry
**Subtopics:** exothermic and endothermic reactions, enthalpy changes, bond energies, energy profiles.
Exothermic reactions release heat to surroundings; endothermic reactions absorb heat. Breaking bonds requires energy; forming bonds releases energy. An approximate reaction enthalpy using bond energies is energy required to break bonds minus energy released on forming bonds.
**Check yourself:** Interpret an energy profile and determine the sign of an enthalpy change.

## 14. Water and Hydrogen
**Subtopics:** properties of water, hardness, purification, hydrogen preparation and uses.
Water is polar and forms hydrogen bonds, contributing to its relatively high boiling point. Temporary hardness is commonly caused by hydrogencarbonates and can be removed by boiling; permanent hardness commonly involves calcium or magnesium sulfates/chlorides and may require other treatment. Hydrogen is a light gas and burns in oxygen to form water.
**Check yourself:** Compare temporary and permanent hardness.

## 15. Air, Oxygen and Nitrogen
**Subtopics:** air composition, oxygen preparation and uses, nitrogen, oxides, pollution.
Air is mainly nitrogen and oxygen, with smaller amounts of argon, carbon dioxide and variable water vapour. Oxygen supports combustion but is not itself a fuel. Nitrogen is relatively unreactive at ordinary conditions because of the strong triple bond in N₂. Nitrogen oxides and sulfur dioxide contribute to air pollution and acid deposition.
**Check yourself:** Explain why nitrogen is relatively unreactive and list sources of air pollution.

## 16. Carbon and Its Compounds
**Subtopics:** allotropes, hydrocarbons, homologous series, functional groups, isomerism.
Diamond and graphite are carbon allotropes with different structures and properties. Hydrocarbons contain carbon and hydrogen only. Alkanes are saturated; alkenes contain a carbon–carbon double bond. Members of a homologous series share a functional group and general formula, with gradual changes in physical properties.
**Check yourself:** Distinguish alkanes from alkenes using structure and a chemical test.

## 17. Organic Nomenclature and Reactions
**Subtopics:** naming simple organic compounds, combustion, substitution, addition, cracking, polymerisation.
Identify the longest appropriate carbon chain and number it to give functional groups or substituents the lowest suitable locants. Alkanes commonly undergo substitution; alkenes undergo addition across the double bond. Complete combustion of hydrocarbons forms carbon dioxide and water; incomplete combustion can produce carbon monoxide or soot.
**Check yourself:** Predict products of simple addition and combustion reactions.

## 18. Petroleum and Petrochemicals
**Subtopics:** crude oil, fractional distillation, cracking, reforming, fuels, pollution.
Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons. Fractional distillation separates fractions by boiling range rather than producing pure single compounds. Cracking breaks larger hydrocarbons into smaller, more useful molecules, including alkenes. Fuel choice involves energy output, availability, cost and environmental effects.
**Check yourself:** Explain the difference between fractional distillation and cracking.

## 19. Metals and Their Compounds
**Subtopics:** reactivity series, extraction, corrosion, alloys, properties and uses.
A metal's extraction method depends on its reactivity. Very reactive metals are commonly extracted by electrolysis; less reactive metals may be reduced from ores using carbon or other reducing agents. Rusting of iron requires oxygen and water; protective coatings, painting, galvanising and alloying can reduce corrosion.
**Check yourself:** Relate the reactivity series to extraction and corrosion prevention.

## 20. Non-metals and Their Compounds
**Subtopics:** halogens, sulfur, phosphorus, chlorine, ammonia, oxides and common uses.
Non-metals often form covalent compounds, though they can form ions in suitable reactions. Halogen reactivity generally decreases down Group 17. Ammonia is alkaline in water and is used in fertiliser manufacture. Chlorine can disinfect water, but must be handled carefully because it is toxic.
**Check yourself:** Explain the displacement trend among halogens.

## 21. Chemical Industries and Environmental Chemistry
**Subtopics:** fertilisers, cement, glass, soap, water treatment, waste, greenhouse effect and acid rain.
Industrial chemistry converts raw materials into useful products. Fertilisers supply plant nutrients; excessive use can cause eutrophication. Greenhouse gases absorb outgoing infrared radiation, while sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides can contribute to acid rain. Pollution control includes efficient processes, treatment of waste and responsible resource use.
**Check yourself:** Link a pollutant to its source, effect and control.

## 22. Qualitative Analysis
**Subtopics:** gas tests, flame tests, tests for anions and cations, observations and inference.
Tests must be reported as observation plus inference. Hydrogen gives a squeaky pop with a lit splint; oxygen relights a glowing splint; carbon dioxide turns limewater milky; ammonia turns damp red litmus blue. Use standard ion tests carefully and do not infer a compound from one inconclusive observation.
**Check yourself:** Memorise gas tests and practise recording precise observations.

## 23. Quantitative Chemistry and Laboratory Skills
**Subtopics:** titration, standard solutions, apparatus, significant figures, graphs, safety and error.
Read a burette at eye level and record initial and final readings. Concordant titres are close results suitable for averaging according to the practical instructions. Use correct units, repeat measurements where possible and distinguish precision from accuracy. Wear appropriate protection and never taste laboratory chemicals.
**Check yourself:** Calculate titre, mean titre and concentration from a balanced equation.

## 24. Nuclear Chemistry
**Subtopics:** isotopes, radioactivity, alpha, beta and gamma radiation, half-life, applications and safety.
Unstable nuclei emit radiation. Alpha particles are relatively strongly ionising and weakly penetrating; gamma radiation is less ionising and highly penetrating. Half-life is the time taken for the number of undecayed nuclei or activity to fall to half its initial value. Radiation has medical and industrial uses but requires careful control.
**Check yourself:** Calculate remaining activity after whole half-life intervals.

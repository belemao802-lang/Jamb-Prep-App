# Physics — Topic-by-Topic Lessons
**Use:** JAMB UTME revision. These are original notes; check topic coverage against the current official JAMB syllabus.

## 1. Measurements and Units
**Subtopics:** SI base and derived units, prefixes, significant figures, scalar and vector quantities, dimensions.
Common SI base units include metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K) and mole (mol). Speed is scalar; velocity is vector. Derived units combine base units, for example force in newtons (N = kg m s⁻²). Convert units before calculations and report sensible significant figures.
**Check yourself:** Distinguish mass from weight and scalar from vector.

## 2. Position, Distance and Displacement
**Subtopics:** reference point, distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration, motion graphs.
Distance is total path length; displacement is directed change in position. Average speed = distance/time; average velocity = displacement/time. Acceleration is change in velocity per time. The gradient of a displacement–time graph gives velocity; the gradient of a velocity–time graph gives acceleration, and area under it gives displacement.
**Check yourself:** Interpret gradients and areas on motion graphs.

## 3. Scalars, Vectors and Equilibrium
**Subtopics:** vector addition, components, resultant, moments, equilibrium.
Vectors have magnitude and direction. Perpendicular components can be combined using Pythagoras' theorem. For equilibrium, resultant force and resultant moment are zero. Moment = force × perpendicular distance from pivot.
**Check yourself:** Resolve a vector into horizontal and vertical components.

## 4. Newton's Laws and Momentum
**Subtopics:** inertia, force, mass, acceleration, action–reaction, impulse, conservation of momentum.
Newton's second law gives resultant force (F=ma) for constant mass. Action and reaction act on different bodies. Momentum (p=mv); impulse equals change in momentum. In an isolated system, total momentum is conserved during collisions.
**Check yourself:** Solve a simple collision using momentum conservation.

## 5. Work, Energy and Power
**Subtopics:** work, kinetic and potential energy, conservation of energy, power, efficiency.
Work done by a constant force along displacement is (W=Fscos\theta). Kinetic energy is (E_k=\frac12mv^2); gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface is (E_p=mgh). Power is work per time. Efficiency = useful output energy/input energy × 100%.
**Check yourself:** Calculate energy changes and efficiency.

## 6. Simple Machines
**Subtopics:** levers, pulleys, inclined planes, mechanical advantage, velocity ratio, efficiency.
Mechanical advantage = load/effort; velocity ratio = distance moved by effort/distance moved by load. Efficiency = MA/VR × 100%. Real machines lose energy, often through friction, so efficiency is below 100%.
**Check yourself:** Explain why a machine can multiply force but not create energy.

## 7. Elasticity and Properties of Matter
**Subtopics:** density, pressure, Hooke's law, stress, strain, Young's modulus.
Density = mass/volume. Pressure = force/area. Within the elastic limit, Hooke's law states extension is proportional to force. Stress = force/cross-sectional area; strain = extension/original length. Young's modulus = stress/strain.
**Check yourself:** Use consistent units in density and pressure calculations.

## 8. Fluids and Pressure
**Subtopics:** liquid pressure, atmospheric pressure, Pascal's principle, buoyancy, Archimedes' principle, flotation.
Pressure in a stationary liquid increases with depth: (p=\rho gh) for gauge pressure. Upthrust equals the weight of displaced fluid. An object floats when its weight is balanced by upthrust. Hydraulic systems transmit pressure through enclosed fluids.
**Check yourself:** Explain why an object can float in water.

## 9. Heat and Temperature
**Subtopics:** thermometers, thermal expansion, specific heat capacity, latent heat, conduction, convection, radiation.
Temperature measures how hot or cold a body is; heat is energy transferred because of temperature difference. Heat energy for a temperature change is (Q=mc\Delta T); during a phase change, (Q=mL). Conduction, convection and radiation transfer thermal energy by different mechanisms.
**Check yourself:** Distinguish specific heat capacity from specific latent heat.

## 10. Gas Laws and Kinetic Theory
**Subtopics:** Boyle's law, Charles' law, pressure law, absolute temperature, molecular motion.
Gas particles move randomly and collide with container walls, producing pressure. For fixed mass at constant temperature, (P_1V_1=P_2V_2). At constant pressure, (V/T) is constant; at constant volume, (P/T) is constant. Use kelvin for gas-law temperatures.
**Check yourself:** Identify what stays constant in each gas law.

## 11. Waves and Wave Motion
**Subtopics:** transverse and longitudinal waves, wavelength, frequency, period, amplitude, wave speed, reflection and refraction.
Waves transfer energy without net transport of matter. Wave speed (v=f\lambda), and period (T=1/f). Transverse waves vibrate perpendicular to propagation; longitudinal waves vibrate parallel to it. Reflection changes direction at a boundary; refraction occurs when wave speed changes.
**Check yourself:** Calculate wavelength from frequency and speed.

## 12. Sound
**Subtopics:** production, propagation, pitch, loudness, echo, resonance, ultrasound.
Sound is a mechanical wave and needs a medium. Frequency relates to pitch; amplitude relates to loudness. An echo is reflected sound. Distance to a reflecting surface can be found from (d=vt/2) when the measured time is for the sound's round trip.
**Check yourself:** Explain why sound cannot travel through a vacuum.

## 13. Light and Geometrical Optics
**Subtopics:** rectilinear propagation, reflection, refraction, mirrors, lenses, total internal reflection, optical instruments.
Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection, measured from the normal. Refraction is bending caused by a change in speed. Refractive index (n=c/v). Convex lenses converge parallel rays; concave lenses diverge them. Ray diagrams help determine image position, nature and orientation.
**Check yourself:** Draw ray diagrams for a plane mirror and a converging lens.

## 14. Dispersion and Optical Phenomena
**Subtopics:** prism, spectrum, colour, scattering, diffraction, interference and polarisation.
White light can disperse into a spectrum because wavelengths refract by different amounts. Diffraction is spreading around edges or through openings; it is more noticeable when opening size is comparable to wavelength. Interference results from superposition of waves.
**Check yourself:** Distinguish dispersion from diffraction.

## 15. Electrostatics
**Subtopics:** charge, conductors and insulators, charging, electric field, Coulomb's law, potential.
Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. Charge is measured in coulombs. Coulomb force magnitude varies with the product of charges and inversely with square of separation. Electric field describes force per unit positive test charge.
**Check yourself:** Explain charging by friction, contact and induction.

## 16. Current Electricity
**Subtopics:** current, potential difference, resistance, Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, electrical power.
Current (I=Q/t); potential difference is energy transferred per unit charge. Ohm's law (V=IR) applies to an ohmic conductor at constant physical conditions. Series resistances add; parallel resistors obey (1/R_p=1/R_1+1/R_2+\dots). Power (P=VI=I^2R=V^2/R).
**Check yourself:** Compare current and voltage in series and parallel circuits.

## 17. Electrical Energy and Domestic Wiring
**Subtopics:** electrical energy, kilowatt-hour, fuses, circuit breakers, earthing, safety.
Electrical energy (E=Pt). One kilowatt-hour is the energy used by a 1 kW device in one hour. Fuses and circuit breakers interrupt excessive current; earthing provides a low-resistance safety path for fault current. Never handle exposed live wiring.
**Check yourself:** Calculate energy cost from power, time and tariff.

## 18. Magnetism and Electromagnetism
**Subtopics:** magnetic fields, permanent magnets, current-carrying conductors, electromagnets, force on a conductor.
Magnetic field lines run from north to south outside a magnet. Current produces a magnetic field. An electromagnet can be strengthened by increasing current or turns and by using a suitable core. A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field can experience a force.
**Check yourself:** State factors affecting electromagnet strength.

## 19. Electromagnetic Induction and Transformers
**Subtopics:** induced emf, Faraday's law, Lenz's law, generators, transformers, energy losses.
Changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces emf. The induced effect opposes the change producing it. A transformer uses alternating current and mutual induction; for an ideal transformer (V_p/V_s=N_p/N_s). Step-up transformers raise voltage and step-down transformers lower it.
**Check yourself:** Explain why a simple transformer does not operate with steady direct current.

## 20. Atomic and Nuclear Physics
**Subtopics:** atomic models, isotopes, radioactivity, alpha, beta and gamma radiation, half-life, fission and fusion.
Radioactive decay is spontaneous and random. Alpha radiation is strongly ionising but weakly penetrating; gamma is highly penetrating. Half-life is the time for undecayed nuclei or activity to halve. Nuclear fission splits a heavy nucleus; fusion combines light nuclei.
**Check yourself:** Calculate remaining activity after a number of half-lives.

## 21. Electronics and Semiconductor Basics
**Subtopics:** conductors, insulators, semiconductors, diodes, rectification, transistors, logic.
Semiconductors have conductivity between conductors and insulators and can be controlled by doping. A diode conducts mainly in one direction and can rectify alternating current. Transistors can act as switches or amplifiers. Learn circuit symbols and the function of each component.
**Check yourself:** Describe the role of a diode in a rectifier.

## 22. Energy Resources and Environmental Physics
**Subtopics:** renewable and non-renewable energy, solar, hydro, wind, fossil fuels, efficiency, pollution.
Renewable sources replenish naturally on useful timescales; non-renewable fuels are finite. Compare energy sources by reliability, cost, efficiency, emissions and environmental impact. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, but useful energy becomes less available in real transfers.
**Check yourself:** Compare advantages and limitations of two energy sources.

## 23. Practical Physics and Data Analysis
**Subtopics:** apparatus, measurements, tables, graphs, gradients, uncertainties, safety.
Record units and instrument precision. Plot independent variable on the horizontal axis and dependent variable on the vertical axis. Use a large graph and a best-fit line when appropriate; gradient is change in vertical value divided by change in horizontal value. Repeat measurements and identify sources of uncertainty.
**Check yourself:** Determine a gradient and explain how repeated measurements improve reliability.

# Computer Studies

## 1. Computer System
A computer accepts data, processes it according to instructions, stores it and produces information. Hardware is the physical equipment; software is the programs and instructions. People and procedures also form part of an information system.

## 2. Data and Information
Data are raw facts; information is data processed into a meaningful form. Accuracy, relevance, timeliness and completeness influence information quality. Input, processing, output and storage are common stages of data processing.

## 3. Hardware and Peripherals
Input devices include keyboards and scanners; output devices include monitors and printers. The CPU executes instructions and coordinates operations. RAM is volatile working memory, while secondary storage retains data without power.

## 4. Software and Operating Systems
System software manages hardware and provides a platform for applications. An operating system manages processes, memory, files, devices and user interaction. Application software helps users perform tasks such as word processing and calculations.

## 5. Networks and Internet
A computer network connects devices to share data and resources. LANs cover a limited area; WANs cover wider areas. The internet is a global network of networks. A browser accesses web content, while a search engine helps find it.

## 6. Cybersecurity and Responsible Use
Use strong unique passwords, multi-factor authentication and updates. Phishing attempts trick users into revealing information. Backups reduce data-loss risk. Respect privacy, copyright and school or workplace rules when using digital resources.

## Original practice questions
1. The physical components of a computer are called
A. software | B. hardware | C. firmware only | D. data
Answer: B. Hardware refers to the physical components of a computer.

2. Which memory is normally volatile?
A. ROM | B. RAM | C. DVD | D. flash drive
Answer: B. RAM generally loses its contents when power is removed.

3. A program that manages hardware and files is the
A. operating system | B. spreadsheet only | C. web page | D. scanner
Answer: A. The operating system manages resources and provides services for applications.

4. A fraudulent message designed to steal login details is often called
A. formatting | B. phishing | C. compiling | D. indexing
Answer: B. Phishing tricks users into revealing sensitive information.

5. Raw facts before processing are called
A. information | B. data | C. knowledge only | D. output
Answer: B. Data are raw facts that can be processed into information.

# Insurance

## 1. Meaning and Principles of Insurance
Insurance transfers specified financial risks to an insurer under a contract in exchange for a premium. Key principles in many general insurance contracts include insurable interest, utmost good faith, indemnity, contribution and subrogation, subject to the policy and applicable law.

## 2. Premium and Policy
The premium is the payment for insurance cover. A policy states the cover, exclusions, limits, conditions and claim process. A proposal form provides information used in underwriting; applicants should answer questions honestly and accurately.

## 3. Types of Insurance
Life assurance provides benefits linked to a person's life and policy terms. Motor, fire, marine, health and property insurance cover different risks. The policy wording determines what events are covered and which exclusions apply.

## 4. Risk Management and Claims
Risk management identifies hazards, estimates likelihood and impact, and selects controls such as avoidance, reduction, transfer or acceptance. A claim should be reported promptly with accurate evidence and documentation, following the policy's procedures.

## 5. Underwriting and Reinsurance
Underwriting assesses risk and decides whether to accept it and on what terms. Reinsurance allows an insurer to transfer part of its risk to another insurer, helping manage large exposures and maintain capacity.

## 6. Insurance Markets and Regulation
Insurance markets connect policyholders, insurers, brokers and agents. Regulation aims to promote solvency, fair treatment and confidence. Consumers should compare cover and exclusions, not premiums alone, and verify that providers are authorised.

## Original practice questions

2. The document setting out cover and exclusions is the

3. Reinsurance is used by insurers to

4. A proposal form should be completed

5. Risk reduction aims to

# Office Practice

## 1. Office Organisation
An office receives, records, processes, stores and communicates information. Tasks may be organised by function or department. Clear responsibilities, procedures and records help staff work efficiently and maintain accountability.

## 2. Filing and Records Management
Filing systems may be alphabetical, numerical, chronological or subject-based. A good system makes records easy to retrieve, protects confidentiality and follows retention rules. Indexing and consistent naming improve retrieval.

## 3. Business Correspondence
Business letters and emails should be clear, courteous, accurate and appropriate to the recipient. Include a meaningful subject, required details and a suitable closing. Proofread names, dates, attachments and figures before sending.

## 4. Meetings and Minutes
Meetings need a purpose, agenda and relevant participants. Minutes record decisions, actions and key points rather than every word spoken. They should identify responsible persons and deadlines where applicable.

## 5. Office Equipment and Technology
Printers, scanners, photocopiers and computers support office tasks. Follow safe operating instructions, protect confidential documents and report faults. Digital files should be backed up and access limited to authorised users.

## 6. Communication and Customer Service
Communication may be written, oral or non-verbal. Effective office communication is accurate, timely and respectful. Customer enquiries should be acknowledged, recorded and directed to the appropriate person while safeguarding personal information.

## Original practice questions
1. The official record of a meeting is called
A. minutes | B. agenda only | C. invoice | D. circular saw
Answer: A. Minutes record the meeting's decisions and important proceedings.

2. A filing system that arranges names from A to Z is
A. chronological | B. alphabetical | C. numerical | D. geographical
Answer: B. Alphabetical filing organises records by letters or names.

3. Before sending an email with an attachment, check that
A. the correct file is attached | B. the subject is always blank | C. all recipients are strangers | D. the message has no purpose
Answer: A. Checking the attachment prevents missing or incorrect files being sent.

4. An agenda is prepared mainly to
A. outline items for discussion | B. record every word after the meeting | C. replace all correspondence | D. calculate wages
Answer: A. An agenda lists the items planned for a meeting.

5. Confidential records should be accessed by
A. any visitor | B. authorised persons only | C. all customers | D. no responsible staff
Answer: B. Access should be limited to people authorised to use the information.

# Store Management

## 1. Store Functions and Layout
A store receives, identifies, protects and issues materials. Layout should support safe movement, easy identification, efficient picking and appropriate environmental conditions. Valuable, hazardous or temperature-sensitive goods may need additional controls.

## 2. Stock Records and Documentation
Goods received notes record deliveries; issue notes document materials released. Bin cards track quantities at a storage location, while stores ledgers may record quantities and values. Records should be updated promptly and reconciled with physical stock.

## 3. Stock Levels and Re-ordering
Minimum, maximum and re-order levels help avoid shortages and excessive stock. Re-order level considers expected usage during lead time and safety stock. Economic order quantity models balance ordering costs against holding costs under stated assumptions.

## 4. Stocktaking and Inventory Control
Stocktaking compares physical quantities with records and investigates discrepancies. First-in, first-out issues older stock first and can be useful for perishable items. Secure access, clear authorisation and regular checks reduce loss and errors.

## 5. Purchasing and Supplier Relations
Purchasing defines requirements, obtains quotations, evaluates suppliers and places authorised orders. Decisions consider quality, price, delivery reliability, service and ethical standards, not just the lowest quoted price.

## 6. Safety and Handling
Use suitable lifting methods, equipment and protective measures. Keep aisles clear, label hazards, store incompatible materials separately and follow fire and emergency procedures. Report damaged goods and unsafe conditions promptly.

## Original practice questions
1. A goods received note records
A. items delivered to the store | B. staff leave only | C. meeting attendance | D. annual profit
Answer: A. A goods received note documents goods received from a supplier.

2. The re-order level helps determine
A. when to place a replenishment order | B. employee salaries only | C. the colour of packaging | D. annual tax rates
Answer: A. Re-order level signals when stock should be replenished, considering usage and lead time.

3. FIFO means
A. first in, first out | B. final invoice, final order | C. fast inventory for offices | D. fixed issue, fixed output
Answer: A. FIFO issues the oldest received stock first.

4. A physical stocktake is used to
A. compare actual stock with records | B. replace every supplier | C. remove all documentation | D. set staff uniforms
Answer: A. Stocktaking identifies differences between physical stock and recorded balances.

5. A store aisle should be kept clear mainly for
A. safe access and movement | B. hiding damaged items | C. blocking emergency exits | D. reducing record accuracy
Answer: A. Clear aisles support safe movement and emergency access.

# Building Construction

## 1. Building Materials
Common materials include concrete, steel, timber, masonry units, glass and roofing products. Material selection depends on strength, durability, cost, availability, fire performance and exposure to weather. Follow applicable standards and design specifications.

## 2. Foundations and Groundworks
Foundations transfer building loads safely to the ground. Selection depends on soil conditions, loads, water table and nearby structures. Excavations require safe support and inspection; foundation work should follow approved drawings and engineering advice.

## 3. Walls, Floors and Roofs
Walls enclose or support spaces, floors carry imposed loads and roofs protect buildings from weather. Structural systems vary. Moisture control, ventilation, insulation, drainage and correct detailing influence building performance.

## 4. Drawings and Specifications
Plans show horizontal layout; elevations show external faces; sections reveal internal relationships. Dimensions, levels, scale, symbols and notes must be read together. Specifications describe materials, workmanship and quality requirements.

## 5. Services and Building Safety
Buildings may contain water supply, drainage, electrical systems and ventilation. Services must be installed by qualified people and comply with applicable safety rules. Site safety includes protective equipment, barriers, housekeeping and safe access.

## 6. Construction Sequence and Quality
A typical project progresses through site preparation, foundations, structural work, enclosure, services, finishes and inspection, though sequence varies. Quality control checks materials, dimensions, workmanship and compliance with approved documents.

## Original practice questions

A. transfer building loads safely to the ground | B. decorate the roof | C. replace all columns | D. store office files

2. A section drawing is especially useful for showing

3. Which material is commonly used as reinforcement in concrete?

4. Before entering a construction site, a worker should

5. Building specifications mainly describe

# Electrical Installation

## 1. Electrical Safety and Protection
Electricity can cause shock, burns and fire. Isolate supplies before work, verify isolation with suitable equipment, use correct protective devices and follow approved procedures. Installation and repair should be performed by qualified people under applicable regulations.

## 2. Conductors, Insulators and Cables
Conductors such as copper allow current to flow; insulators resist current. Cables have conductors and insulation suited to voltage, current, environment and installation method. Correct cable sizing helps prevent overheating and excessive voltage drop.

## 3. Circuits and Wiring
A circuit provides a complete path for current. Domestic loads are commonly connected in parallel so each receives the supply voltage and can operate independently. Wiring must follow the specified circuit design and local electrical standards.

## 4. Earthing and Protective Devices
Earthing connects exposed conductive parts to an earth system to help limit dangerous touch voltages and support fault protection. Fuses and circuit breakers interrupt excessive current; residual-current devices provide additional protection against certain earth-leakage faults.

## 5. Tools, Testing and Maintenance
Use insulated tools where required and inspect equipment before use. Testing may include continuity, insulation resistance, polarity and protective-device checks, performed with suitable instruments by competent personnel. Record results and correct faults before energising.

## 6. Electrical Symbols and Drawings
Electrical drawings use standard symbols to show supplies, switches, outlets, lighting points and protective equipment. Read the legend, circuit labels and drawing notes. Never infer a safe connection from a symbol alone without checking the full design and standards.

## Original practice questions
1. The primary purpose of earthing is to
A. help provide a safe path for fault current and reduce touch-voltage risk | B. increase current in every circuit | C. replace all insulation | D. store electricity permanently
Answer: A. Earthing supports protective measures against dangerous fault voltages.

2. A fuse protects a circuit by
A. melting and interrupting excessive current | B. increasing voltage without limit | C. storing charge for days | D. replacing the switch
Answer: A. A fuse element melts when excessive current exceeds its rating for sufficient time.

3. In a parallel domestic circuit, appliances generally
A. operate independently at the supply voltage | B. must all be switched on together | C. share one current in every branch | D. cannot be switched separately
Answer: A. Parallel branches receive the supply voltage and can operate independently.

4. Before repairing a circuit, the supply should be
A. isolated and verified safe | B. left live for convenience | C. covered with paper only | D. connected to water
Answer: A. Safe isolation and verification are essential before electrical work.

5. A cable's current rating depends partly on
A. conductor size and installation conditions | B. the owner's name | C. the colour of the room only | D. the price of the switch only
Answer: A. Conductor size, insulation, ambient temperature and installation method affect current capacity.

# Use of English — Multiple-Choice Practice Bank
**Question type:** Newly written practice, not official JAMB past questions.  
**Instructions:** Choose A, B, C or D. Answers and explanations are provided after each item for study. For a test version, copy only the question and options, then keep the answer key separately.

## Topic 1: Comprehension and Summary

**1.** A writer's main idea is best described as  
A. the longest sentence in the passage  
B. the central point the passage develops  
C. an unfamiliar word in the passage  
D. the reader's personal opinion  
**Answer: B.** The main idea is the central point supported by the passage.

**2.** Which is the best practice when writing a summary?  
A. Add personal opinions to make it persuasive  
B. Copy every example and repeated detail  
C. State the essential points concisely in your own words  
D. Introduce information from another source  
**Answer: C.** A summary preserves essential meaning while removing repetition and unnecessary details.

## Topic 2: Lexis and Structure

**3.** Choose the word that correctly completes the sentence: “The committee will ___ a decision tomorrow.”  
A. do  
B. make  
C. create up  
D. perform  
**Answer: B.** “Make a decision” is the standard collocation.

**4.** Which word correctly completes the sentence? “Her careful ___ prevented the error.”  
A. analyse  
B. analysis  
C. analytical  
D. analytically  
**Answer: B.** A noun is required as the subject of “prevented”.

## Topic 3: Antonyms and Synonyms

**5.** Choose the word nearest in meaning to *reluctant*.  
A. unwilling  
B. eager  
C. careless  
D. certain  
**Answer: A.** “Reluctant” means unwilling or hesitant.

**6.** Choose the word opposite in meaning to *scarce*.  
A. rare  
B. limited  
C. abundant  
D. small  
**Answer: C.** “Abundant” means plentiful, the opposite of scarce.

## Topic 4: Sentence Interpretation

**7.** “She barely passed the examination” means that she  
A. did not take the examination  
B. passed by a small margin  
C. achieved the highest score  
D. refused to receive her result  
**Answer: B.** “Barely” indicates that the result was only just sufficient.

**8.** “Unless Musa apologises, I will not speak to him” means that I will speak to Musa  
A. even if he never apologises  
B. only if he apologises  
C. before he has done anything  
D. because he has already apologised  
**Answer: B.** “Unless” introduces a condition; an apology is required before the speaker will speak to him.

## Topic 5: Idioms and Phrasal Verbs

**9.** If a speaker says “Let us break the ice,” the speaker wants to  
A. damage something frozen  
B. make people feel more comfortable  
C. end the meeting immediately  
D. start an argument  
**Answer: B.** “Break the ice” means ease tension or begin friendly interaction.

**10.** In the sentence “The nurse looked after the patient,” *looked after* means  
A. searched for  
B. stared at  
C. took care of  
D. waited behind  
**Answer: C.** “Look after” is a phrasal verb meaning take care of.

## Topic 6: Parts of Speech

**11.** What is the grammatical class of *quickly* in “The runner moved quickly”?  
A. Noun  
B. Adjective  
C. Adverb  
D. Preposition  
**Answer: C.** “Quickly” modifies the verb “moved”.

**12.** In which sentence is *light* used as a verb?  
A. The light is bright.  
B. Please light the candle.  
C. This bag is light.  
D. We saw a traffic light.  
**Answer: B.** “Light” describes the action of setting the candle burning.

## Topic 7: Concord and Agreement

**13.** Choose the correct sentence.  
A. The basket of oranges are heavy.  
B. The basket of oranges is heavy.  
C. The basket of oranges were heavy today.  
D. The basket of oranges have heavy.  
**Answer: B.** The subject is the singular noun “basket”; “of oranges” does not change the subject's number.

**14.** Everyone in the classrooms ___ ready.  
A. are  
B. were being  
C. is  
D. have  
**Answer: C.** “Everyone” is grammatically singular and takes “is” in this sentence.

## Topic 8: Tenses and Sequence of Tenses

**15.** By the time we arrived, the film ___.  
A. begins  
B. has begun tomorrow  
C. had begun  
D. will begin  
**Answer: C.** The past perfect marks an event completed before another past event.

**16.** She has lived in Port Harcourt ___ 2022.  
A. for  
B. since  
C. during  
D. by  
**Answer: B.** “Since” introduces the starting point; “for” introduces a duration.

## Topic 9: Question Tags

**17.** “They have finished the work, ___?”  
A. haven't they  
B. have they  
C. don't they  
D. didn't they  
**Answer: A.** A positive statement takes a negative tag using the auxiliary “have”.

**18.** “Let's begin the lesson, ___?”  
A. will we  
B. shall we  
C. do we  
D. aren't we  
**Answer: B.** The conventional tag after “Let's” is “shall we?”

## Topic 10: Punctuation and Spelling

**19.** Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.  
A. After the rain stopped we continued, our journey.  
B. After the rain stopped, we continued our journey.  
C. After, the rain stopped we continued our journey.  
D. After the rain stopped we, continued our journey.  
**Answer: B.** The introductory clause is followed by a comma, and no comma separates the verb from its object.

**20.** Which sentence uses the apostrophe correctly?  
A. The teachers' room belongs to several teachers.  
B. The teacher's are in class.  
C. The teachers room's are open.  
D. The teachers's room is open.  
**Answer: A.** The plural possessive “teachers'” shows that the room belongs to several teachers.

## Topic 11: Register and Word Choice

**21.** Which word is most appropriate in a formal medical report?  
A. tummy ache  
B. feeling rough  
C. abdominal pain  
D. a bad belly  
**Answer: C.** “Abdominal pain” is a standard formal medical expression.

**22.** Which expression is most suitable for a formal application letter?  
A. “I wanna join your school.”  
B. “I wish to apply for admission.”  
C. “Put me in, please.”  
D. “Your place looks cool.”  
**Answer: B.** It is clear, formal and appropriate to the purpose.

## Topic 12: Oral English — Vowels

**23.** A diphthong is a vowel sound that  
A. has no sound at all  
B. moves from one vowel quality toward another within one syllable  
C. is always written with two separate syllables  
D. can occur only at the beginning of a word  
**Answer: B.** A diphthong involves a change in vowel quality during one syllable.

**24.** Why should pronunciation questions not be answered by spelling alone?  
A. Every letter always represents two sounds  
B. English spelling always follows one fixed sound rule  
C. The same letter or letter group can represent different sounds  
D. Vowels never occur in spoken English  
**Answer: C.** English spelling-to-sound relationships are not always one-to-one.

## Topic 13: Oral English — Consonants

**25.** The sounds /p/ and /b/ are both normally classified as  
A. bilabial plosives  
B. dental fricatives  
C. velar nasals  
D. palatal approximants  
**Answer: A.** Both are produced with the two lips and a complete closure; /p/ is voiceless and /b/ voiced.

**26.** Which feature distinguishes /s/ from /z/ in standard English pronunciation?  
A. Place of articulation only  
B. Voicing  
C. Number of syllables  
D. Word meaning in every context  
**Answer: B.** /s/ is voiceless while /z/ is voiced; both are alveolar fricatives.

## Topic 14: Stress and Intonation

**27.** In standard pronunciation, which pair commonly illustrates a stress difference between a noun and a verb?  
A. REcord (noun) / reCORD (verb)  
B. table / chair  
C. green / blue  
D. fast / quickly  
**Answer: A.** The noun commonly has first-syllable stress, while the verb commonly has second-syllable stress.

**28.** Sentence stress is mainly used to  
A. remove all vowel sounds  
B. highlight important or contrasting information  
C. change every noun into a verb  
D. make every sentence a question  
**Answer: B.** Stress can signal focus, contrast and emphasis.

## Topic 15: Cloze Passages

**29.** Choose the best connector: “The road was flooded; ___, the team arrived on time.”  
A. therefore  
B. nevertheless  
C. because  
D. for example  
**Answer: B.** “Nevertheless” signals contrast between the flooded road and arriving on time.

**30.** Choose the word that best completes the sentence: “Although Tunde was tired, he ___ working until the task was complete.”  
A. persisted in  
B. prevented  
C. depended  
D. objected  
**Answer: A.** “Persisted in working” means continued despite difficulty.

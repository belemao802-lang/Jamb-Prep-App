# Chemistry — Original Multiple-Choice Practice Bank
**Instructions:** Choose the best answer (A–D). These are newly written practice questions, **not official JAMB past questions**.

## Questions
1. Which method is best for separating an insoluble solid from water? A. Filtration B. Fractional distillation C. Chromatography D. Sublimation
2. Which method can separate coloured dyes in ink? A. Filtration B. Chromatography C. Decantation only D. Crystallisation only
3. The atomic number of an element equals its number of A. neutrons B. protons C. protons plus neutrons D. shells
4. Isotopes of an element have the same number of A. neutrons B. nucleons C. protons D. protons and neutrons combined
5. Elements in the same group often have similar chemical properties because they have similar A. numbers of occupied shells B. outer-shell electron arrangements C. neutron numbers D. atomic masses
6. Across a period, atomic radius generally decreases mainly because A. nuclear charge increases B. atoms lose all electrons C. neutron number becomes zero D. the nucleus disappears
7. Ionic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between A. neutral molecules B. oppositely charged ions C. two neutrons D. shared electron pairs only
8. Solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity well because its ions A. are absent B. are fixed in a lattice C. have no charge D. become gases
9. When balancing a chemical equation, you should change A. subscripts in formulae B. coefficients before formulae C. element symbols D. charges arbitrarily
10. How many moles are in 10 g of a substance with molar mass 20 g mol⁻¹? A. 0.5 mol B. 2 mol C. 10 mol D. 200 mol
11. At constant temperature, Boyle's law relates pressure and volume by A. P/V = constant B. PV = constant C. P + V = constant D. P = V²
12. A temperature of 27°C is approximately A. 27 K B. 246 K C. 300 K D. 373 K
13. A solution contains 0.20 mol solute in 0.50 dm³ solution. Its concentration is A. 0.10 mol dm⁻³ B. 0.40 mol dm⁻³ C. 2.5 mol dm⁻³ D. 4.0 mol dm⁻³
14. Which expression is commonly used for a simple dilution? A. C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ B. C₁ + V₁ = C₂ C. C/V = 1 D. C₁C₂ = V₁V₂
15. Neutralisation of an acid by a base commonly produces A. salt and water B. oxygen and hydrogen only C. metal and carbon D. glucose
16. Which solution is alkaline? A. One with pH 2 B. One with pH 7 exactly at 25°C C. One with pH 11 D. One with pH 0
17. Oxidation, in terms of electrons, is A. gain of electrons B. loss of electrons C. gain of neutrons D. loss of protons from every atom
18. An oxidising agent is a substance that A. accepts electrons and is reduced B. donates electrons and is oxidised C. never changes D. stops all reactions
19. During electrolysis, cations move towards the A. anode B. cathode C. salt bridge only D. power switch
20. A catalyst increases reaction rate mainly by A. raising activation energy B. providing a lower-activation-energy pathway C. being permanently consumed D. changing all products
21. Increasing temperature usually increases reaction rate because particles A. stop moving B. collide more energetically and more may exceed activation energy C. lose all kinetic energy D. become atoms of a new element
22. At dynamic equilibrium in a closed system, the forward and reverse reaction rates are A. both zero B. equal C. always unequal D. unrelated to concentration
23. A catalyst added to an equilibrium mixture generally A. changes the equilibrium constant B. speeds attainment of equilibrium without changing its position C. stops the reverse reaction D. changes the balanced equation
24. An exothermic reaction A. absorbs heat from surroundings B. releases heat to surroundings C. cannot occur spontaneously D. always requires electricity
25. Breaking chemical bonds generally A. releases energy B. requires energy C. creates no energy change ever D. removes all atoms
26. Temporary hardness of water is commonly associated with A. calcium and magnesium hydrogencarbonates B. sodium chloride only C. pure oxygen D. glucose
27. Which gas relights a glowing splint? A. Carbon dioxide B. Oxygen C. Ammonia D. Hydrogen chloride
28. Nitrogen gas is relatively unreactive at room conditions partly because N₂ has A. a weak single bond B. a strong triple bond C. no electrons D. an ionic lattice
29. Which hydrocarbon is unsaturated? A. Ethane B. Methane C. Ethene D. Propane
30. A homologous series has members with the same A. molecular mass B. functional group and general formula C. physical state at all temperatures D. number of carbon atoms
31. Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon produces A. carbon dioxide and water B. carbon and hydrogen only C. ammonia and salt D. nitrogen and sulfur
32. Fractional distillation of crude oil separates fractions mainly by differences in A. colour only B. boiling ranges C. atomic number D. acidity
33. Cracking is used to A. join all small molecules into metals B. break larger hydrocarbons into smaller molecules C. remove all carbon from petroleum D. turn water into crude oil
34. Rusting of iron requires A. oxygen and water B. nitrogen alone C. carbon dioxide alone in every case D. sunlight only
35. Very reactive metals such as sodium are commonly extracted by A. simple filtration B. electrolysis of suitable molten compounds C. crystallising seawater D. chromatography
36. Down Group 17, halogen reactivity generally A. increases B. decreases C. remains exactly identical D. becomes zero immediately
37. Excess fertiliser entering a lake may cause A. eutrophication B. elimination of all algae forever C. no biological change D. formation of pure oxygen only
38. Which gas turns limewater milky? A. Hydrogen B. Carbon dioxide C. Oxygen D. Nitrogen
39. Which gas turns damp red litmus paper blue? A. Ammonia B. Oxygen C. Carbon dioxide D. Hydrogen
40. A burette is commonly used to A. measure a variable volume accurately in titration B. measure mass directly C. heat a solid to fusion D. determine atomic number
41. If a radioactive sample has an initial activity of 80 units, what remains after two half-lives? A. 60 units B. 40 units C. 20 units D. 10 units
42. Which radiation is generally the most penetrating of alpha, beta and gamma? A. Alpha B. Beta C. Gamma D. All are identical
43. Which particle has a negative charge? A. Proton B. Neutron C. Electron D. Alpha particle
44. A salt can often be prepared from an acid and an insoluble base by adding excess base, then A. filtering and crystallising the solution B. adding more insoluble base forever C. discarding the liquid immediately D. freezing the mixture only
45. Which statement about bond energies is correct? A. Bond breaking releases energy B. Bond formation releases energy C. Both always absorb energy D. Neither involves energy
46. Which is a sensible laboratory safety practice? A. Taste unknown chemicals B. Smell every gas directly C. Wear suitable protection and follow instructions D. Mix unknown reagents to see what happens
47. The formula of magnesium chloride is A. MgCl B. MgCl₂ C. Mg₂Cl D. Mg₂Cl₃
48. Which process converts a liquid into vapour from its surface below the boiling point? A. Freezing B. Evaporation C. Condensation D. Sublimation

## Answer Key and Explanations
1. **A** — Filtration retains insoluble solid while liquid passes through.
2. **B** — Chromatography separates dyes by their different movement.
3. **B** — Atomic number is proton number.
4. **C** — Isotopes have equal proton numbers and different neutron numbers.
5. **B** — Similar valence-shell arrangements produce related chemical behaviour.
6. **A** — Increasing nuclear attraction generally pulls electrons closer across a period.
7. **B** — Ionic bonding is attraction between oppositely charged ions.
8. **B** — The ions are not free to move in the solid lattice.
9. **B** — Coefficients balance atom counts without changing chemical identities.
10. **A** — (n=m/M=10/20=0.5) mol.
11. **B** — For a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature, PV is constant.
12. **C** — Kelvin ≈ Celsius + 273, so 27°C ≈ 300 K.
13. **B** — Concentration = 0.20/0.50 = 0.40 mol dm⁻³.
14. **A** — The relation accounts for the same amount of solute before and after dilution.
15. **A** — Acid-base neutralisation commonly forms salt and water.
16. **C** — At about 25°C, pH above 7 is alkaline.
17. **B** — Oxidation is electron loss.
18. **A** — The oxidising agent accepts electrons and is reduced.
19. **B** — Positive cations are attracted to the negative cathode.
20. **B** — A catalyst provides a pathway with lower activation energy.
21. **B** — Higher temperature increases particle kinetic energy and the effective collision fraction.
22. **B** — Dynamic equilibrium has equal forward and reverse rates.
23. **B** — A catalyst speeds both directions and does not alter equilibrium position.
24. **B** — Exothermic reactions release heat.
25. **B** — Energy is needed to overcome the attraction holding a bond together.
26. **A** — Temporary hardness commonly comes from hydrogencarbonates.
27. **B** — Oxygen relights a glowing splint.
28. **B** — The strong N≡N bond requires substantial energy to break.
29. **C** — Ethene contains a carbon–carbon double bond.
30. **B** — Homologous members share a functional group and general formula.
31. **A** — Complete combustion forms carbon dioxide and water.
32. **B** — Fractions condense over different boiling ranges.
33. **B** — Cracking produces smaller hydrocarbons, often including alkenes.
34. **A** — Rusting requires both oxygen and water.
35. **B** — Highly reactive metals are extracted by electrolysis rather than carbon reduction.
36. **B** — Halogen reactivity generally falls down the group.
37. **A** — Nutrient enrichment can cause algal growth and oxygen depletion.
38. **B** — Carbon dioxide forms a milky calcium carbonate precipitate in limewater.
39. **A** — Ammonia is alkaline in water and turns damp red litmus blue.
40. **A** — A burette delivers and measures volume precisely during titration.
41. **C** — 80 → 40 → 20 units after two half-lives.
42. **C** — Gamma radiation is generally the most penetrating.
43. **C** — Electrons carry negative charge.
44. **A** — Filtration removes excess insoluble base; crystallisation recovers the salt.
45. **B** — Bond formation releases energy; bond breaking requires energy.
46. **C** — Protective equipment and safe procedures reduce risk.
47. **B** — Mg²⁺ requires two Cl⁻ ions for a neutral compound.
48. **B** — Evaporation occurs at a liquid's surface below its boiling point.

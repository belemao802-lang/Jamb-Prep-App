# Technical Drawing — Multiple-Choice Practice Bank
**All questions are newly written practice questions, not official JAMB past questions.** Answers and explanations follow the questions.

## Instruments and Construction
**1.** Which instrument is most suitable for drawing a circle of a specified radius?  
A. Compass  B. T-square  C. Set square  D. Scale rule

**2.** A perpendicular bisector meets a line segment at  
A. 30°  B. 45°  C. 90°  D. 180°

**3.** The sum of the interior angles of a hexagon is  
A. 360°  B. 540°  C. 720°  D. 900°

**4.** The locus of points a fixed distance from one fixed point is a  
A. Straight line  B. Circle  C. Parabola only  D. Rectangle

## Scales and Dimensioning
**5.** A scale of 1:50 means 2 cm on the drawing represents  
A. 25 cm  B. 50 cm  C. 100 cm  D. 1,000 cm

**6.** Which line is normally used to indicate an edge hidden from view?  
A. Dashed line  B. Thick continuous line  C. Freehand border  D. Dimension line

**7.** Dimension figures are used mainly to show  
A. The colour of a part  B. The required size  C. The weight of paper  D. The drawing's author only

## Projection
**8.** Orthographic projection represents an object using  
A. One photograph  B. Views projected perpendicular to planes  C. Shading only  D. A single perspective view

**9.** In first-angle projection, the plan is normally placed  
A. Above the front view  B. Below the front view  C. Inside the title block  D. To the far right in every case

**10.** In third-angle projection, the plan is normally placed  
A. Above the front view  B. Below the front view  C. Inside the front view  D. Under the title block

**11.** An isometric drawing conventionally has its two receding axes at about  
A. 15°  B. 30°  C. 45°  D. 60° to the horizontal

**12.** In an oblique drawing, the front face is usually shown  
A. In true shape  B. As a circle in every case  C. Without edges  D. At random scale

## Sections and Developments
**13.** Section lining is used to identify  
A. Surfaces cut by the section plane  B. All empty spaces  C. Dimensions only  D. Hidden edges only

**14.** The lateral development of a cylinder is a  
A. Triangle  B. Rectangle  C. Circle  D. Trapezium

**15.** The length of the rectangular lateral development of a cylinder of diameter d is  
A. d/2  B. 2d  C. πd  D. πd²

## Interpretation and Application
**16.** An auxiliary view is especially useful for showing the true shape of  
A. An inclined surface  B. A title block  C. A dimension arrow  D. A hidden line only

**17.** A floor plan is generally a view looking  
A. Horizontally at the roof  B. Down onto the building layout  C. Up from the foundation  D. Only at the front elevation

**18.** An assembly drawing mainly shows  
A. How components fit together  B. Only one isolated material sample  C. A list of colours  D. A map of the site only

**19.** A parts list may include component names and  
A. Item numbers and quantities  B. Weather reports  C. Exam scores  D. Unrelated paragraphs

**20.** Before interpreting a technical drawing, you should check the  
A. Title block, scale and projection symbol  B. Paper colour only  C. Artist's favourite colour  D. Number of eraser marks

## Answer Key and Explanations
1. **A** — A compass draws circles and arcs with a set radius.  
2. **C** — A perpendicular bisector crosses the segment at a right angle.  
3. **C** — (6−2)×180°=720°.  
4. **B** — All points at fixed distance r from a centre form a circle.  
5. **C** — 2 cm×50=100 cm in reality.  
6. **A** — Dashed lines conventionally represent hidden edges.  
7. **B** — Dimensions communicate required lengths and sizes.  
8. **B** — Orthographic views are projected perpendicular to projection planes.  
9. **B** — In first-angle projection, the plan is below the front view.  
10. **A** — In third-angle projection, the plan is above the front view.  
11. **B** — The receding isometric axes are conventionally at 30° to the horizontal.  
12. **A** — Oblique drawing normally keeps the front face in true shape.  
13. **A** — Hatching indicates material intersected by the cutting plane.  
14. **B** — Unrolling the cylinder's side produces a rectangle.  
15. **C** — The circumference is πd, which becomes the rectangle's length.  
16. **A** — An auxiliary projection can show an inclined face in true size and shape.  
17. **B** — A floor plan is a top-down representation of the layout.  
18. **A** — An assembly drawing explains the relationship between assembled parts.  
19. **A** — Parts lists commonly identify items and quantities.  
20. **A** — These drawing details establish scale, orientation and projection conventions.

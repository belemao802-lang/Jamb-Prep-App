# Remaining JAMB Subject Packs — Starter Notes and Practice

These are original starter materials. Language packs require review by fluent subject teachers and should be checked against the exact current syllabus and prescribed texts.

# Hausa

## 1. Karatu da Fahimtar Rubutu
Reading comprehension tests the ability to identify the main idea, supporting details, tone and implied meaning in a Hausa passage. Read the full passage, locate evidence for each answer and avoid choosing an option that adds information not present in the text.

## 2. Nahawu da Tsarin Jumla
Study the grammatical roles of words, sentence order, agreement and the way meaning changes with word choice. Use the current prescribed syllabus and a fluent Hausa reference for examples and exceptions.

## 3. Kalmomi da Ma'anoni
Vocabulary questions may test synonyms, antonyms, context, idioms and proverbs. Learn a word with an example sentence and note whether its meaning changes in different contexts.

## 4. Adabi da Karin Magana
Oral and written literature can include stories, poetry, drama, proverbs and oral traditions. Explain a proverb by identifying its intended lesson and the situation in which it is used.

## 5. Rubutu da Harshen Baka
Practise spelling, punctuation, clear written expression and spoken-language conventions. Pay attention to the prescribed orthography and any tone or pronunciation marks required by the course.

## Original practice questions
1. A comprehension answer should be based mainly on
A. evidence in the passage | B. a guess unrelated to the text | C. the longest option | D. personal preference
Answer: A. Comprehension answers should be supported by the passage.

2. A proverb is commonly used to express
A. a lesson or general observation | B. a mathematical unit only | C. a list of names only | D. a punctuation mark
Answer: A. Proverbs communicate concise lessons or observations.

3. When studying a new Hausa word, a useful method is to
A. learn its meaning in a sentence | B. memorise its shape only | C. ignore context | D. change its spelling randomly
Answer: A. A sentence shows how the word is used in context.

# Igbo

## 1. Ịgụ na Nghọta
Reading comprehension involves finding the central idea, details, purpose and implied meaning in an Igbo text. Read carefully and support each answer with evidence from the passage rather than outside assumptions.

## 2. Ụtọasụsụ na Nhazi Ahịrịokwu
Study parts of speech, sentence construction, word relationships and how grammar shapes meaning. Check examples against the prescribed syllabus and a fluent Igbo language reference, especially where dialect and orthography matter.

## 3. Okwu na Ihe Ọ Pụtara
Vocabulary includes meaning in context, related words, opposites, idioms and proverbs. Record new words with example sentences and review their usage in different contexts.

## 4. Edemede na Agụmagụ
Written and oral literature may include folktales, poetry, drama and proverbs. Discuss theme, characters, setting and lesson, and use evidence from the text to support interpretation.

## 5. Nsụpe na Nkwupụta
Practise standard spelling, punctuation, clear writing and pronunciation. Follow the orthography and language conventions specified by the current course materials.

# Arabic

## 1. Reading and Comprehension
Read the complete Arabic passage before answering. Identify the main idea, supporting information, references and implied meaning. Use the context to distinguish similar words and support answers with evidence from the text.

## 2. Grammar: Nouns, Verbs and Particles
Arabic grammar distinguishes nouns, verbs and particles. Sentence structure, case endings where taught, agreement and verb forms affect meaning. Learn grammatical rules with examples from the prescribed text and syllabus.

## 3. Vocabulary and Word Patterns
Arabic vocabulary includes roots, derived forms, synonyms, antonyms and contextual meanings. A root may connect words in meaning, while patterns can change grammatical role or shade of meaning.

## 4. Writing and Orthography
Arabic is written from right to left. Practise letter forms in their positions, joining rules, spelling and punctuation conventions. Diacritics can indicate short vowels and grammatical information in learning texts.

## 5. Literature and Expression
Study prescribed prose, poetry and texts for theme, style, imagery and purpose. Summarise accurately, distinguish literal from figurative language and support interpretation with evidence.

## Original practice questions

2. Arabic grammar commonly distinguishes nouns, verbs and
A. particles | B. paragraphs only | C. punctuation marks only | D. page numbers
Answer: A. A traditional basic classification includes nouns, verbs and particles.

3. A comprehension response should be grounded in
A. the passage | B. unrelated personal opinion | C. the longest option | D. a guess
Answer: A. Reading questions should be answered from textual evidence.

# French

## 1. Compréhension écrite
Read the full French text and identify its subject, key details, purpose and implied meaning. Use context and grammatical clues to infer unfamiliar vocabulary, and verify that the selected answer fits the whole sentence.

## 2. Grammaire et structure
Review articles, gender and number, adjective agreement, pronouns, negation, questions and common verb forms. French verbs change according to subject and tense; learn high-frequency patterns with example sentences.

## 3. Vocabulaire et expressions
Learn vocabulary in topic groups such as family, school, food, travel and daily routines. Study common expressions in context and pay attention to accents because spelling can affect meaning and correctness.

## 4. Temps verbaux
Practise the present, passé composé, imparfait and futur proche at the level required by the syllabus. Choose a tense based on the time relationship and meaning, not by translating word-for-word from English.

## 5. Rédaction et communication
Write short, coherent messages with a clear purpose, suitable greeting and closing. Check agreement, verb forms, accents, punctuation and word order. Use connectors to make ideas flow logically.

# Music

## 1. Elements of Music
Core elements include pitch, rhythm, melody, harmony, dynamics, tempo, timbre and texture. Describe how these elements shape a piece rather than simply stating whether it sounds pleasant or unpleasant.

## 2. Notation and Rhythm
Staff notation places notes on lines and spaces. Clefs identify pitch ranges; note values and rests indicate duration. Time signatures organise beats into measures, while bar lines divide measures.

## 3. Instruments and Families
Instruments may be grouped by how they produce sound, such as strings, woodwind, brass, percussion and keyboards. Identify the instrument's sound source and role in an ensemble.

## 4. Musical Forms and Ensembles
Musical form describes how sections are organised, such as binary, ternary, verse-chorus or theme and variations. Ensembles combine instruments or voices, requiring balance, timing and attentive listening.

## 5. African and Nigerian Music
Study regional musical traditions, instruments, performance contexts and the roles music plays in ceremonies, storytelling, worship and community life. Avoid treating all Nigerian traditions as identical; describe the specific tradition in the question.

## Original practice questions
1. Tempo refers to the
A. speed of the beat | B. loudness only | C. instrument material | D. lyrics' meaning only
Answer: A. Tempo describes how fast or slow the beat moves.

2. Dynamics in music describe
A. loudness and softness | B. the number of pages | C. the instrument's price | D. the title only
Answer: A. Dynamics indicate changes or levels of loudness.

3. A time signature helps show
A. beats organised in each measure | B. the singer's age | C. the concert venue only | D. the instrument's colour
Answer: A. A time signature indicates the metrical organisation of beats.

# Fine Arts

## 1. Elements and Principles of Art
Elements include line, shape, form, colour, texture, space and value. Principles include balance, contrast, emphasis, rhythm, unity, proportion and movement. Explain how an artist uses these features to organise a composition.

## 2. Drawing and Observation
Observe proportion, contour, light, shadow, perspective and spatial relationships. Begin with light construction lines, compare relative sizes and refine details only after the main structure is accurate.

## 3. Colour Theory
Hue identifies a colour family; value describes lightness or darkness; saturation describes intensity. Complementary colours sit opposite each other on a colour wheel and can create strong contrast. Colour effects also depend on surrounding colours and light.

## 4. Design and Composition
Composition arranges visual elements within a space. Balance may be symmetrical or asymmetrical; emphasis directs attention, while repetition can create rhythm. Consider audience, purpose, materials and constraints.

## 5. Art History and Cultural Context
Artworks reflect individual choices and cultural, historical and social contexts. Describe observable features first, then interpret meaning with evidence. Distinguish personal preference from a supported analysis of the work.

## Original practice questions
1. Which is an element of art?
A. line | B. taxation | C. inflation | D. temperature only
Answer: A. Line is a basic visual element of art.

2. The lightness or darkness of a colour is its
A. value | B. tempo | C. mass | D. pitch
Answer: A. Value describes how light or dark a colour appears.

3. A composition's emphasis helps
A. direct the viewer's attention | B. remove every contrast | C. make all objects identical | D. replace observation
Answer: A. Emphasis establishes a focal point or area of importance.

# Home Economics

## 1. Nutrition and Balanced Diet
Nutrients include carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals and water. A balanced diet provides appropriate amounts for a person's needs. Requirements vary with age, activity, health and life stage; food safety and variety are important.

## 2. Food Preparation and Safety
Use clean hands, safe water and clean equipment. Separate raw foods from ready-to-eat foods, cook foods thoroughly and store them at safe temperatures. Follow recipes carefully and prevent cross-contamination.

## 3. Clothing and Textiles
Textiles differ in fibre, weave, durability, absorbency and care requirements. Read care labels, choose suitable fabrics for the purpose and use safe methods when measuring, cutting, sewing and pressing garments.

## 4. Family Resource Management
Time, money, energy and household materials are limited resources. Planning, budgeting, prioritising needs and comparing value help households make informed decisions. A budget should reflect realistic income and expenses.

## 5. Child Development and Home Care
Child development includes physical, cognitive, language, social and emotional growth. Safe environments, responsive care, hygiene, play and suitable nutrition support development. Household care should consider individual needs and safety.

## Original practice questions
1. A balanced diet provides
A. appropriate amounts of essential nutrients | B. only carbohydrates | C. no water | D. the same quantity for every person in every situation
Answer: A. A balanced diet supplies essential nutrients in amounts suited to individual needs.

2. To reduce cross-contamination, raw meat should be
A. kept separate from ready-to-eat foods | B. stored on top of uncovered cooked food | C. handled with the same unwashed board | D. left at room temperature indefinitely
Answer: A. Separating raw meat from ready-to-eat foods helps prevent pathogen transfer.

3. A household budget helps to
A. plan income and expenditure | B. guarantee unlimited income | C. remove the need to compare prices | D. replace food safety
Answer: A. A budget plans how available income will meet expected expenses.

# Physical and Health Education

## 1. Fitness and Health
Health-related fitness includes cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility and body composition. Appropriate activity should be regular, progressive and suited to a person's age, ability and health.

## 2. Rules and Skills in Sport
Learn the basic rules, skills, positions and safety requirements of the sports in the syllabus. Good performance combines technique, decision-making, teamwork, fair play and respect for officials and opponents.

## 3. Training Principles
Training principles include specificity, overload, progression, reversibility, recovery and individual differences. Increase training demands gradually and allow recovery. Warm-ups prepare the body for activity but cannot guarantee that injury will never occur.

## 4. Nutrition, Hygiene and First Aid
Hydration, balanced nutrition, sleep and personal hygiene support health and performance. First aid involves prompt, appropriate assistance while seeking professional help when needed. Follow recognised safety guidance and do not attempt procedures beyond your training.

## 5. Human Body and Exercise
The cardiovascular and respiratory systems supply oxygen and nutrients during activity. Muscles produce movement through coordinated contraction, while bones and joints provide support and leverage. Exercise responses vary with intensity, fitness and health.

# Electronics

## 1. Basic Electronic Components
Resistors limit or control current, capacitors store electric charge, and diodes conduct mainly in one direction. Transistors can switch or amplify signals. Component ratings and circuit context determine correct use.

## 2. Current, Voltage and Resistance
Current is charge flow per unit time; voltage is energy transferred per unit charge; resistance opposes current. Ohm's law V=IR applies to ohmic conductors under constant physical conditions.

## 3. Semiconductors and Diodes
Semiconductors have electrical properties between conductors and insulators. A diode is commonly used for rectification and protection. Light-emitting diodes produce light when forward biased within their ratings.

## 4. Digital Logic
Digital circuits represent information using discrete states, commonly 0 and 1. Logic gates such as AND, OR and NOT perform Boolean operations. Truth tables show the output for each possible input combination.

## 5. Circuit Testing and Safety
Use the correct meter range and connection method. Switch off power before changing circuit connections unless the test specifically requires an energised circuit and is performed safely by a competent person. Avoid short circuits and observe component polarity.

## Original practice questions
1. A resistor is mainly used to
A. limit or control current | B. produce unlimited energy | C. store fuel | D. measure distance
Answer: A. A resistor provides resistance and can control current or divide voltage.

2. A diode normally conducts most readily in
A. one direction | B. both directions equally in every case | C. no direction under any condition | D. a direction chosen by colour
Answer: A. A standard diode conducts mainly when forward biased.

3. The unit of electrical resistance is the
A. ohm | B. watt | C. coulomb | D. tesla
Answer: A. Resistance is measured in ohms (Ω).

# Metalwork

## 1. Workshop Safety
Identify hazards before work, wear suitable protective equipment, secure workpieces and use machine guards. Keep the work area clean and report damaged tools. Follow the teacher's or supervisor's instructions and never bypass safety devices.

## 2. Measuring and Marking Out
Steel rules, squares, scribers, punches and measuring tools help transfer dimensions accurately. Read the correct scale, mark reference edges and check measurements before cutting. Precision at the marking stage reduces waste.

## 3. Hand Tools and Machines
Files remove small amounts of material; hacksaws cut metal; drills make holes; hammers and punches support marking and forming. Select the correct tool, secure the work and use equipment only for its intended purpose.

## 4. Joining and Fabrication
Metal components may be joined by welding, brazing, soldering, riveting or mechanical fasteners. Choose a process based on material, load, access, finish and safety requirements. Heat-based processes require ventilation and suitable protection.

## 5. Materials and Finishing
Metals differ in strength, hardness, ductility, corrosion resistance and workability. Finishing may include filing, polishing, painting or protective coatings. Store materials safely and dispose of swarf and waste appropriately.

## Original practice questions
1. Before operating workshop machinery, a learner should
A. check guards and follow safety procedures | B. remove every guard | C. wear loose clothing near moving parts | D. ignore the instructions
Answer: A. Safety checks, guards and suitable clothing reduce workshop hazards.

2. A hacksaw is commonly used to
A. cut metal | B. measure voltage | C. test soil pH | D. polish glass only
Answer: A. A hacksaw cuts metal stock using a toothed blade.

3. Marking out is done before cutting mainly to
A. transfer dimensions accurately | B. increase waste | C. hide the workpiece | D. replace measurement
Answer: A. Accurate marking helps cuts and holes match the drawing.

# Woodwork

## 1. Wood and Timber
Timber properties include grain, moisture content, strength, durability and workability. Seasoning reduces moisture to improve stability. Select timber based on the intended use, load, appearance and exposure conditions.

## 2. Measuring and Marking Tools
Rules, tape measures, try squares, marking gauges and pencils transfer dimensions and reference lines. Check measurements from a consistent datum and mark clearly before cutting or drilling.

## 3. Hand Tools and Machine Safety
Saws cut, planes smooth surfaces, chisels remove material and drills make holes. Secure workpieces, use sharp tools correctly and follow machine guards and operating instructions. Never distract a person using machinery.

## 4. Joints and Assembly
Wood joints include butt, lap, mortise-and-tenon and dovetail joints. Choose a joint based on load, appearance, material and construction method. Accurate marking and fitting improve strength and alignment.

## 5. Finishing and Workshop Practice
Sanding prepares surfaces; finishes can protect timber and improve appearance. Remove dust, apply products according to instructions and provide ventilation. Keep the workshop tidy and handle finishes and waste safely.

## Original practice questions
1. A try square is mainly used to check or mark
A. right angles | B. electrical current | C. liquid volume | D. magnetic poles
Answer: A. A try square checks and marks 90-degree angles.

2. Seasoning timber is intended mainly to
A. reduce moisture content | B. add water indefinitely | C. remove all grain | D. turn wood into metal
Answer: A. Seasoning reduces moisture and improves timber stability.

3. A dovetail joint is commonly used in
A. woodworking | B. electrical earthing | C. concrete testing only | D. map reading
Answer: A. Dovetail joints are used in woodworking, often for strong corner joints.
